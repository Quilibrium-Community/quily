# Community Recap — #general

**September 23, 2026** | 12:40 AM UTC – 10:17 AM UTC

## From Cassie

Cassie addressed the community about response delays, clarifying that sometimes she simply requires sleep after extended work sessions (up to 72 hours in a flow state). She asked the community not to jump to speculation about her wellbeing until a reasonable time has passed.

She provided a substantive update on the shard merge/split fix, noting that the presumed fix for transactions over shard merges has required adjustment but is on the right path. Cassie explained the technical challenge: the merge case is harder than split because the same provers can appear on both sides of the merge, requiring the global prover shard information to be moved deeper into the consensus stack. She clarified that app consensus steps differ from the core consensus algorithm itself.

In response to concerns about code auditing, nearly 600 lines of code, Cassie noted the codebase has been reduced drastically, and said "Two waves" - first during the Rust conversion, then when switching from HotStuff consensus to Commonware. She also mentioned she would clear out old Go code before version 0.25 merges.

## Testing the Merge Fix

Follow-up on the discussion about progress. Cassie explained the current state involves a full testnet evaluation run. If changeset wrong, the testnet is re-run, and this cycle continues until all logs is correct.

## Consensus Architecture Explained

When asked about the difficulty of the consensus changes, Cassie gave a detailed technical breakdown of the core "app consensus" is being augmented with merge/split awareness, particularly around the prover economics and cross-shard transaction, and merging issues.

## Code Volume & Audit

When asked about code auditing, Cassie described the volume of the codebase and the millions of lines of code, which had been dramatically reduced. A community member suggested libraries that the token price will increase once adoption and continues building.

## Community Discussions on Support

Several members expressed appreciation for Cassie's work, and a community discussed her own health.

A long conversation about the project's market position, including community members discussing the lack of price action, the amount of liquidity, and the potential for an improvement.
