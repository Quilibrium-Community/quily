# Community Recap — #general

**October 5, 2026** | 12:10 AM UTC – 10:05 AM UTC

## From Cassie

Cassie released an update (`.25`) addressing recent findings, with the main focus on fixing proof submission and network acceptance — the core issue blocking rewards. She clarified that shard splits/merges and proof publishing are independent subsystems: "one can fail while the other succeeds." She noted that average block times have improved from several minutes down to 32 seconds, calling it "a nice change," and confirmed sharding appears to be working. She also addressed the remaining major item: rewards resolution, saying "things are trending positive, and this should be fast to resolve." Other items on the list include cluster mode improvements and RPC roundtrip latency.

On the wQUIL "mintable" flag on DexScreener, Cassie explained: ownership renounced would never apply to a wrapped token contract (needed for security upgrades), mintable is how wrapped tokens work, and the "suspicious functions" flag is unclear since the contract is a fully audited ERC-20 with OpenZeppelin's MintWithSignature addition. She contacted go+ (the security scanner), who removed the more egregious warnings but left these in place, noting they're technically accurate contract properties.

## Network Status & Rewards

Community members observed shard splits occurring and average block times hitting 10.0 seconds — the first time seen at that exact value. Cassie confirmed splits are working but emphasized that rewards depend on proof submission and network acceptance, not splits themselves. She remains hopeful.

## Recovery Issue #664

Black Swan reported that recovery issue #664 remains the main blocker, with 434 certificate rejections and 432 `NotReady` results logged across 12 workers on Oct 5. They raised a key question: which authenticated historical membership source should recovery use? They also flagged historical archive reads (#672) as potentially blocking the recovery path, with another reporter reproducing subtree read failures across five archives. Black Swan plans to run a store-wiping experiment and share evidence. Cassie said she's looking into it and will report back when `.25` is updated.

## wQUIL "Mintable" Flag Discussion

Community members discussed why DexScreener flags wQUIL as "mintable." Mr.Quil shared a QUILY assistant response explaining that wQUIL is a mint-on-bridge ERC-20 controlled by a T-of-N MPC signer — no single party has unilateral minting authority. The flag appears because the contract contains a mint-capable function, which is normal for wrapped tokens. Cassie's explanation (above) confirmed this, and 0xjos thanked her for the clarification.
