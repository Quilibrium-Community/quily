# Community Recap — #general

**November 16, 2026** | 12:20 AM UTC – 1:55 PM UTC

## Quilibrium Release Progress

Cassie clarified the status of the upcoming releases, noting that nothing has been explicitly stated about .25. She confirmed that .121 showed what they wanted to see, and they're publishing .122 to add extra overhead room for proofs to alleviate strain. To the best of her knowledge, .122 is the one — the proof system improvement will come in a .26 update, but .25 appears to be approaching gold master state.

## Bandwidth, Starlink, and Node Economics

A discussion emerged about Starlink's potential role in Quilibrium's future. Cassie pushed back on the idea that Starlink pricing is competitive, noting they've been raising prices and imposing strong usage limits. However, if Starlink achieves ideal coverage with better pricing, it would be a net win — Quilibrium is already built to capitalize on it, since earning Q is largely achieved by available bandwidth. She emphasized that bandwidth is always the resource in scarce supply on the internet.

Community members shared their own connectivity situations, with one noting Starlink at $52.50/month for 100Mbps versus $138/month for 10.1Mbps from a traditional ISP. Another member running nodes on Starlink reported consistent 40Mbps up/down and asked about latency and routing issues affecting cert share. Cassie mentioned they're getting at least one of the archives an IPv6 address to resolve an unrelated issue.

## LLM Weight Auditing and Open Weights

Cassie revisited a previous concern about LLMs potentially being backdoored with malicious code. She shared research showing that Astra could natively read Qwen's weights and fix reasoning loops, and that while sycophantic behavior couldn't be detected from raw weights, it could be detected by reading activation patterns. She suggested this creates precedence for detecting anomalous model behaviors, potentially enabling a "MoMoE" (mixture of mixtures of experts) approach where multiple models from different sources prevent any single compromise.

On the topic of open weights, Cassie stated firmly: "This is why I think weights should be open. And why I'm deeply suspicious of those who don't want them to be." She shared a fair use ruling on AI training and books.

## Node Resource Scaling

A community member asked how node requirements scale with network growth, citing 40+ Mbps usage and terabytes of disk writes. Black Swan clarified that scaling currently follows the node's own worker count, not total network workers, and that main pressure from network expansion is planned to land on archives. Cassie noted that writes don't imply storage use — repeated writes over the same data accumulate SSD wear on a predictable timeframe.

## Developer Onboarding

Felix, a new developer, expressed interest in contributing. Cassie suggested that megarpc will obviate the need for a light node version, and proposed several API projects for getting started: improving the slow /token/supply scan mode, adding endpoints for shard split/merge status and earnings impact, and creating a /apps/* endpoint for app discovery with drillable metadata.
