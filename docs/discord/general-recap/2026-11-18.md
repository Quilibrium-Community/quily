# Community Recap — #general

**November 18, 2026** | 2:21 AM UTC – 2:46 PM UTC

## From Cassie

Cassie provided several substantive updates on the network's performance and development:

- **TPS assessment**: Cassie explained that TPS is calculated by taking the entire span of transactions under app shard frames plus any transactions under global context, divided by the frame time (currently targeting ~10 seconds).

- **Archive performance fixes**: Three items were deployed to archives to address slowdowns: re-calculation of addresses, replacing unnecessary RocksDB scans with direct key lookups, and stopping stale executions before prover registry refresh. Initial results showed committed execution time sliced significantly, with execution time past budget dropping from 72% to 26% of cases. A second round of updates added prover refresh optimization, duplicate clock write prevention, and additional logging to isolate remaining slow spots.

- **Node operator impact**: Cassie clarified that the performance fixes will not require regular node operators to update before the official .25 release or .26 thereafter.

- **App Store status**: Quorum Messenger (QM) will be un-shippable to Chinese App Store users due to China's explicit demand for a backdoor in E2EE messenger apps. Cassie stated Quilibrium will not comply. Chinese iOS users can obtain QM via a different region install, sideloading via Xcode, or as a PWA. France also won't be approved initially, requiring documentation of encryption methods (similar to US requirements for closed-source products). The requirement from Apple goes into effect in 2027.

- **Quorum webapp**: Work with the imgnai team is nearing completion for the updated quorum webapp.

- **Network capabilities**: Cassie explained that throughput is directly correlated to shard count and provers per shard, with the network aiming for stable equilibrium. She highlighted QStorage and QKMS as flagship services, noting that a "private, verifiable web" is inevitable given increasing surveillance and supply-chain attacks (citing the recent Optimizely hack affecting Zoom).
