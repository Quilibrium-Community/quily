# Community Recap — #general

**October 30, 2026** | 12:01 AM UTC – 11:08 AM UTC

## Network and Development Updates

Cassie reported that global frames are averaging around 10 seconds, with lattice operations for confidential transfers experiencing slow verification times. She and Black Swan have been diagnosing the issue and identified two key findings:

1. **AVX512 support**: Two of the archives were incorrectly assumed to lack AVX512 support — they actually have it, so those can now receive AVX512 images. Archives were updated with the AVX512 speedup.

2. **Lattice CT code optimization**: The lattice private transaction code isn't using native SIMD and AVX512 intrinsics where it could. Cassie is evaluating the potential speedup, which will determine follow-up actions.

A .112 release is planned to help both validation and proof sides. There is no official .25 binary release yet — the latest signed release is .24. To access shielding (a .25 feature), users need to build from the latest branch or use the quilscan dev node. Community members noted that pre-.25 coins are not lost as long as users have their `.config/` folder and `keys.yml` — they're just unshielded (transparent). Once synced on a .25 build, users can import/shield those old coins.

## From Cassie

Cassie shared her experience working on Coinbase's wallet stack, noting she worked on every part of their cold and hot wallet infrastructure as one of six employees trusted with physical key shards. She praised their security approach as "more extreme than my own cold custody," and shared a Wired article about Coinbase's physical vault system. She noted that at Coinbase, an infected dev machine couldn't unilaterally affect a hot wallet's actions, with strict schema enforcement of signing requests from a separate team and service.

She also addressed the Binance security incident (a compromised DevOps engineer's laptop), calling it "pretty embarrassing for an exchange like Binance." On the AI/quantum speculation around breaking ECC, she emphasized that the real risk isn't AI magically finding a way, but someone operationalizing existing cryptographic vulnerabilities — while noting genie wishes would still require figuring out how to execute.

## Binance Hack Discussion

Community members discussed the Binance security incident involving a compromised DevOps engineer's laptop. The initial investigation found no evidence of wallet private key leaks. Cassie noted the attack vector of compromising a dev machine is common, and that such an incident couldn't happen at Coinbase due to their security architecture. Some users speculated about the response, including CZ's post about employees pulling an all-nighter. Members also discussed North Korean state actors' incentives around crypto hacks.
